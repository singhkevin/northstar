A single line
in flight.

The Northstar ribbon is an "N" drawn as one flight path: it lifts off, folds and climbs. Everything here (colour, type, motion and voice) comes from that idea: technical, calm and in motion.

04 · Imagery

Blue hour, real places.

Footage is graded cool: warm tones are pulled down, shadows go steel blue and highlights stay clean white. We show real-looking hardware at work on sites, in plant rooms and in stairwells. No holograms, no fantasy cities, and no brand logos on the machines.

Drone · graded
Humanoid · graded
  • HUD overlays (brackets, scan lines, tracking boxes) are drawn in code, never baked into footage.
  • Any interface with made-up figures is marked "Illustrative" or "Sample data".
  • No AI-generated people presented as staff, customers or testimonials.
  • Use the ribbon as a watermark at most once per page, at around 5% opacity.
05 · Interface

Soft corners, sharp lines.

Buttons are pills that fill with the Flight gradient on hover. Cards use 14 px corners. Dividers are 1 px, and a short gradient dash marks each section label.

06 · Voice

Plain, specific, calm.

Say what the robot actually does. One idea per headline. Confidence comes from precision, not adjectives.

Say"The quadruped walks Basement B2 every night and flags standing water before the morning shift."
Don't say"Our revolutionary, cutting-edge AI robots are disrupting the future of construction."
  • No invented numbers, customers, logos or testimonials. Capabilities only, until real results exist.
  • Avoid "revolutionary", "next-gen", "cutting-edge" and "disrupt".
  • Never name clients publicly without their OK.
  • Write for site and operations leads: practical outcomes first.
